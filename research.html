Research

Cellular mechanisms of neuronal excitability and neurodegeneration

Our research asks how interactions between neurons, glial cells and the extracellular environment determine whether neural circuits remain functional or progress towards dysfunction and degeneration. We bridge cellular electrophysiology, neuron–glia physiology and experimental neurodegeneration.

01. Neuron–astrocyte interactions and K+ homeostasis

Neurons depend on astrocytes to maintain the extracellular environment required for normal signalling. We investigate how astrocytic K+ clearance shapes neuronal excitability and how disruption of this homeostatic process may contribute to neurological disease.

Scientific thread: astrocytic ion handling → extracellular K+ → neuronal excitability.

02. Excitability profiles and selective neuronal vulnerability

What makes one neuron more susceptible to degeneration than another?

A fundamental question in neurodegeneration is why particular neuronal populations are selectively vulnerable while neighbouring neurons can remain relatively resistant.

The Buskila Lab investigates whether the intrinsic electrophysiological profile of a neuron contributes to this vulnerability. We examine the cellular mechanisms governing resting membrane properties, firing behaviour and excitability, and how these properties change during disease progression.

HCN channels and the Ih current form one component of this broader excitability profile. Rather than treating a single conductance as the defining focus, we consider how multiple intrinsic membrane properties combine to influence excitability, hyperexcitability and neuronal susceptibility.

Scientific thread: intrinsic membrane properties → excitability profile → altered firing/hyperexcitability → selective neuronal vulnerability → neurodegeneration.

03. ALS and motor-neuron vulnerability

ALS provides an experimental context in which we investigate neuronal and glial contributions to vulnerability. We consider how intrinsic excitability profiles interact with astrocytic regulation of the extracellular environment, including K+ homeostasis, during disease progression.

04. Neuroinflammation and restoration of homeostasis

We investigate how inflammatory changes may alter astrocyte function and ion homeostasis, and whether restoring the neuronal environment can preserve physiological function during neurodegeneration.

05. Neurodegenerative and injury models

Our programme applies this mechanistic framework across neurodegenerative and neurological conditions, including ALS, Alzheimer’s disease and brain injury, asking which cellular disturbances are shared and which reflect selective vulnerability in particular neuronal populations.

06. Experimental approaches

We use cellular and network-level physiological approaches to connect membrane properties with neural function, including intracellular electrophysiology, extracellular recordings and measurements of extracellular K+ in experimental tissue preparations.
